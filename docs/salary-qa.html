Cleaning 26,000 real salary survey answers, without losing a row.

The 2021 Ask a Manager salary survey is a public Google Form with 26,232 self-reported responses, typed by real people, in free text. This pipeline standardizes it, applies severity-graded QA rules, checks re-identification risk, codes the open-ended answers and shows how much cleaning changes the answer.

1. What the raw data gets wrong

Raw average salary for USD-paid respondents is $96,659, pulled up by entries such as $102,000,000. After cleaning and excluding robust outliers it is $90,948 (5.9% lower). The median stays at $79,000, which is why the cleaned mean, not the raw one, belongs in a report.

Rejects (276 rows) are duplicates, unparseable or missing currencies, zero salaries and salaries that are clearly typed in thousands or as hourly pay. Everything else is kept with a warning, so analysts decide, not the script. Full exception log: exceptions.csv.

2. Privacy: anonymous is not the same as safe

A regex scan for emails, phones, SSNs, URLs and handles found only 3 URLs; the form did not ask for direct identifiers. The bigger risk is combinations. On job title + city + industry + age band, 99.4% of rows fall in groups smaller than 5. Dropping city still leaves 79.8%. Releasing only industry + age band + country brings it down to 9.8%.

3. What people say about their pay

2,834 respondents added context on income. A market-research style codeframe (multi-code, with negation handling so "no overtime" is not coded as overtime) codes 78.4% of them. A hand review of 100 random code assignments judged 92 correct; the misses and their reasons are in codeframe_audit.csv.

USD-paid respondents, outliers excluded, industries with 300+ answers. Self-selected readers of one blog; not a population estimate.

Method, limits and source

Source: Ask a Manager Salary Survey 2021, republished by the TidyTuesday project (2021-05-18). Respondents self-selected; currency conversion uses approximate 2021 average rates and is not used for the headline numbers. The codeframe is rule-based and transparent; it is a first pass that a coder would extend. Code, tests and full method: GitHub. By Titus Robinson.
